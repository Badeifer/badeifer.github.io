Gemini 4 Hydrogen costs double what 3.8 Flash does, until Flash's price jumps in January

Gemini 4 Hydrogen is Google's new everyday model for coding and agents, priced at $1.50 and $7.50 per million tokens. Here is what it beats, why the per-task bill can still drop, and the API changes that will return errors if you just swap the model name.

AINovember 15, 20264 min read

Google released Gemini 4 Hydrogen on 12 November, the fast, cheap tier of its Gemini 4 family and the model most developers will actually call all day. It is far stronger than Gemini 3.8 Flash, and at $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens it is also exactly twice the price. The part that matters for your budget is on Google's pricing page: on 1 January 2027, 3.8 Flash goes up to the same rates.

So the choice is not really "old and cheap or new and expensive." In seven weeks it becomes "old or new, same price," and you have until then to test the new one before the cheap option disappears.

What Hydrogen is for

Google pitches Hydrogen as the everyday model for coding and agent work, sitting under the two heavier Gemini 4 models, Carbon and Argon. It is the model ID gemini-4-hydrogen in the Gemini API and AI Studio, and it is also live in Gemini for business, Android Studio, Google Search and the Gemini app. Free app users only get it when automatic model selection decides a prompt needs it; choosing it by name takes a paid plan. In Antigravity, Google's agent coding tool, it needs a paid AI Pro or Ultra plan.

It takes up to 1,048,576 tokens, about a million, but that limit is now shared between input and output instead of counted separately. It has four thinking levels: low, medium, high and a new xhigh.

The benchmark numbers are Google's, and they are large. On Terminal-Bench 4.1, a test of agents doing real work in a command line, Hydrogen scores 57% against 18.2% for 3.8 Flash. On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, a composite of several hard evaluations run by an independent firm, Hydrogen scores 51 at high and 54 at xhigh. The best 3.8 Flash variant scores 31. That puts the cheap model narrowly above Argon, which Google lists at 50, and below Carbon.

Two small discrepancies are worth knowing about if you quote these. Google's main announcement says Terminal-Bench 4.1 in one place and 4.0 in another, and Neurohive points out that Google's 55.8% on the Vals Legal Research Bench shows as 53.85% on Vals's own leaderboard.

Why a pricier token can make a cheaper job

Per-token price is only half the bill. The other half is how many tokens the model burns to finish. Artificial Analysis measures what it costs to run each model through one task of its index. At its top setting, xhigh, Hydrogen costs $3.16 per task. 3.8 Flash at its top setting, high, costs $7.77. Hydrogen gets a much better score for well under half the money, because it reaches answers in fewer tokens.

That will not hold for every workload. A short classification call that 3.8 Flash answers in a handful of tokens gains nothing from Hydrogen's efficiency and simply costs twice as much until January. Long agent runs with lots of tool calls are where the savings should show up. Google's partners reported 2 to 2.8 times faster runs and 12% to 23% fewer tokens on SQL and data-heavy jobs, but those are Google's chosen examples.

Cheaper routes exist. Batch and Flex requests cost half, at $0.75 and $3.75. Cached input drops to $0.075 per million tokens. Priority costs $2.70 and $13.50. Free-tier prompts may be used to improve Google's products; paid-tier prompts are not.

What breaks if you only change the model name

This is the practical part, and it is longer than most model swaps. Google's Cloud developer guide lists the changes. The ones most likely to bite:

  • The default thinking level is now high, up from medium on 3.8 Flash. Unless you set it, every call thinks harder and bills more output. Set thinking_level="MEDIUM" to keep the old behaviour while you compare.
  • thinking_budget and thinking_level="MINIMAL" return HTTP 400. Remove the first and pick low instead of the second.
  • temperature, top_p and top_k are silently ignored, while frequency_penalty, presence_penalty and candidate_count above 1 return errors. Use response_schema if you need predictable output.
  • Explicit context caching is gone. cached_content is not supported. Caching is now automatic for prompts of 4,096 tokens or more, so put your fixed instructions and documents at the start of the prompt.
  • Thinking counts toward max_output_tokens at high and xhigh. A limit that worked on 3.8 Flash can now cut answers short.
  • Agent harnesses need care. Editing earlier turns or the system instructions on replay returns a 400 by default; if your tool compacts history, set prefix_mismatch_behavior="DROP". Prefilled model turns are not allowed, and with parallel function calls only the first call carries the thought signature.

Google also calls Hydrogen a short-term release with at least 45 days' notice before retirement, so pin the model ID in code and watch for the notice.

Test now, while you can still compare against a model that costs half.

The bigger picture

The headline price went up, and Google is clearly betting nobody will mind because the work gets done in fewer tokens. That is the shape of model pricing now: the price list tells you less every quarter, and the honest number is cost per finished task on your own workload. Run your real prompts through both models before 1 January. After that, 3.8 Flash stops being the cheap option and becomes just the old one.
